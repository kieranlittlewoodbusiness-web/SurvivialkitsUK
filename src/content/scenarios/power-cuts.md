Start by checking what you already keep at home: a torch, spare batteries, blankets, a radio and a charged power bank. Many households already have most of what a short outage needs.

## Stay informed without draining your phone

Write **105** (England, Scotland and Wales) on paper and keep it with a torch. UK guidance recommends a battery or wind-up radio when broadband drops. A car radio can work, but in severe weather it may be safer to stay indoors.

## Light and warmth

Torches are safer than candles. Stay warm with layered clothing and blankets; close unused rooms and draw curtains. If you or someone you support needs extra help, check eligibility for your supplier’s [Priority Services Register](https://www.thepsr.co.uk/).

## Food, water and devices

Keep bottled water and ready-to-eat food that does not need cooking. Unplug TVs and computers before power returns if surge risk concerns you. Ask telecare or medical-device providers how equipment behaves in an outage—do not assume a consumer power bank is suitable.
